## 1. Context

The team is entering **K-Tech College 2026: AI for Business Innovation Hackathon**, run by LIKELION Vietnam, in the HR & Recruitment track. K-Tech College is a Korean government-funded programme that connects Vietnamese IT talent with Korean companies, so recruitment is directly relevant to the organiser.

The repository holds a pitch-era mockup: `interviewpath/interviewpath-demo` is entirely mock data, and `SpeakCV` is a real but generic single-tenant interview coach. Neither has a company concept, a per-company knowledge graph, or any real HR feature.

The contest requires only an idea and a runnable mockup. The owner knows this and chose to build a **real system on synthetic data**: a working pipeline where every number is computed, running on two fictional Korean companies that are clearly labelled as sample data. The submission deadline is explicitly not a planning constraint.

### Contest requirements that bind this work

- Deliverable: English PDF, at most 15 slides. A prototype or MVP earns bonus points.
- Final pitch: at most 15 minutes in English, with a demo video of at most 2 minutes, then 5 minutes of Q&A.
- Scoring: Problem 25%, Idea & Innovation 30%. The brief contradicts itself on the rest: its table says Feasibility 20% and Presentation 25%, its section headings say 25% and 20%. Ask the organiser.
- The submission link's visible text differs from its target. Confirm the correct link with the organiser before submitting.
- No personal, proprietary or sensitive data in the submission.
- The team must contribute substantively. AI tools are allowed; entries wholly generated by AI are rejected.
- The solution must consider security, reliability and AI risks.

## 2. Product

### Thesis

Other tools treat a company as a text box. This product treats a company as a living model: every hiring step reads from one company knowledge graph, and real outcomes write back to it.

Positioning line: **"Every CV gets a conversation. Every decision has evidence."**

### Problems addressed

1. **CVs are a weaker signal.** Candidates widely use AI to write them, and application volume is rising.
2. **Real requirements live in managers' heads.** A Korean manager thinks in Korean, Vietnamese HR writes the JD, and candidates guess. This is a hypothesis.
3. **Culture mismatch surfaces too late.** This is a hypothesis.

Hypotheses 2 and 3 have no quantitative evidence yet. The team must interview real HR staff at Korean companies before the Problem slide is written.

### Users

| User | App | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnamese HR staff | HR portal | Screen with evidence, write recruiting content, answer policy questions, see which channels work |
| Korean hiring manager | HR portal, Korean pages | State requirements and read candidates in Korean |
| Candidate | SpeakCV | A fair chance beyond the CV, and honest feedback |
| Employee | HR portal | Policy answers and an onboarding plan |

## 3. End-to-end workflow

1. **Candidate uploads a CV** to a job and consents to data processing. The system records the source channel.
2. **AI reads the CV** against the job's requirements, with identity masked, and sets a label: `CV meets` or `CV does not meet`.
3. **Every candidate is invited to SpeakCV**, whatever the label. They first see **Fit Compass**: what matches, what is missing, how the team works. They may withdraw.
4. **Official SpeakCV session**: microphone check, consent to recording, then an AI voice interview. Recording is mandatory. Questions come from the requirements the CV did not show, claims in the CV, culture scenarios, and the actual team's work.
5. **Results go to the HR portal**: recording, transcript, evidence per requirement, and an AI suggestion.
6. **HR gives final approval** on the 2×2 board. Every rejection is an HR action.
7. **Approved candidates meet the Korean manager**, who has a Korean brief and targeted questions, and records a scorecard.
8. **Hired candidates receive an onboarding plan.** Rejected candidates receive personal feedback that HR approved.

### Branches

- Candidate withdraws at Fit Compass: the application closes without an AI session, and the reason is kept for statistics.
- Candidate refuses recording: the application is not auto-rejected. It keeps its CV label and waits for HR with no interview evidence.
- Technical failure mid-session: the session resumes at the current question within 24 hours. HR can grant one restart.
- HR can fast-track an exceptional candidate past the session.

## 4. Core rules

These rules hold across all blocks. Changing one requires the owner's decision.

1. **AI never rejects anyone.** AI may advance and suggest. Only an HR action sets a rejection.
2. **A weak CV still gets the interview.** The application keeps a `CV does not meet` label so HR can tell it apart.
3. **The CV label never changes automatically**, even after a strong interview. Only HR can correct it, with a logged reason.
4. **Candidates never see the words "does not meet".** They see gaps in Fit Compass.
5. **There is no practice mode.** The owner removed it; there is one official session per application.
6. **Recording is mandatory for the official session**, with consent captured first. The default is audio and transcript, no video.
7. **There is no overall score** such as "8.6". HR sees evidence per requirement.
8. **There is no single culture-fit score.** HR reads answers and notes against values the company documented.
9. **No scoring of faces, emotions or accents.** The camera is not recorded or analysed.
10. **No guessing whether a CV was written by AI.** Probing by conversation replaces it.
11. **"Not explained" is not "lied".** The system never concludes dishonesty.
12. **AI output that cites a source must be verified by code** against that source before it is shown.
13. **Numbers are computed by code.** AI may explain a computed table but may not produce figures.
14. **Only HR-approved knowledge is used** by any AI feature.
15. **No AI vendor is the standard.** Business code never names a vendor; see Section 6.

## 5. The HR board

The CV label and the interview evidence form a 2×2 board:

| | Interview: much evidence | Interview: little evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **CV meets** | Priority | Strong CV, unclear answers |
| **CV does not meet** | Hidden Gem | Not enough evidence |

Lanes under the board hold candidates who have not interviewed, refused recording, or withdrew. **Hidden Gem** is the group a conventional CV filter would miss. It is the product's strongest pitch point and is visually emphasised in the UI, which also counters HR bias against the "does not meet" label.

## 6. Architecture

```
HR portal (interviewpath-demo, Vite)  ─┐
                                       ├─ FastAPI (SpeakCV backend) ─ MySQL 8
Candidate app (SpeakCV, Next.js)      ─┘         │
                                                 └─ AI Gateway ─ configurable vendors
```

Decisions:

- **One backend, two frontends.** The HR-shaped UI in `interviewpath-demo` is reused, Caddy already routes three domains, and HR and candidates are separate audiences who need no single sign-on.
- **The graph lives in the existing MySQL.** Queries need only 1 to 2 hops on small data. MySQL 8.0 has no vector type, so embeddings are compared in Python. Retrieval sits behind one module so it can move to pgvector or Neo4j later.
- **n8n is dropped.** Pipelines run as FastAPI background jobs. Interrupted jobs are marked failed at startup and can be re-run, so no Redis or queue is needed.
- **The legacy "AI Brain" and the hardcoded topic graph are replaced.** Only the upload and text-extraction code is reused.
- **AI access goes through a provider-neutral gateway** with five slots: `reasoning`, `bulk`, `embedding`, `stt`, `tts`. Vendor and model come from environment variables. Vendors differ: Anthropic offers no embedding model and lists no speech services, so a Claude setup needs a second vendor. Default endpoints are official. An unofficial proxy may be configured only for sample data, never for real candidate data.

### Vendor choice

The design does not depend on it. Once keys exist, `make eval-brain` runs the same question set on each vendor so the choice is made from measurements. Rough, unmeasured session cost estimates are in the Block 1 spec.

### Languages

UI: English first for the contest, Vietnamese second. Korean appears on manager-facing pages (Block 4) and in interviews. Interviews run in Vietnamese, English or Korean, as the job allows. Copilot answers in the asker's language.

## 9. Non-goals

**Remove from old slides and scripts**, because the product will not do them: n8n automation, Google Calendar sync and automatic scheduling, automatic rejection emails, shadowing and pronunciation practice.

**Not built in this project**: automatic posting to job boards, billing and subscriptions, attrition prediction, video recording, face analysis, OCR for scanned CVs.

**Only if real users appear**: Neo4j or a dedicated vector database, a separate job queue, a mobile app, integration with existing HR systems.

## 10. Honesty constraints for the pitch

- Channel Intelligence and Reality Check need months of real hiring data. On sample companies they run on labelled simulated data and prove the mechanism, not the effect. Slides must say so.
- Competitor claims come from search results and are unverified. Verify each before it reaches a slide.
- The slides may name K-Tech College as a possible deployment path but must not imply a partnership with LIKELION.
- Evaluation targets in the block specs are goals. Only measured results go on slides.
- Legal statements about Vietnam's Law 91/2025/QH15 come from secondary summaries and need checking against the source text. Consent wording and retention periods are proposals, not legal advice.

### Demo video storyboard (2 minutes, timings are targets)

1. **0:00 The manager speaks.** 30 seconds of Korean produces a Vietnamese, English and Korean JD, each line traced to a source.
2. **0:25 A polished AI-written CV.** The AI probes a claim; the answer is logged as "not explained".
3. **0:55 Same CV, two companies (key scene).** A process-heavy conglomerate and an autonomous startup ask visibly different questions.
4. **1:20 HR sees evidence.** Swapping name and gender leaves the result identical. The manager reads a Korean brief. A human decides.
5. **1:45 After hiring.** Copilot reports two conflicting documents. The dashboard shows the channel with the most applications did not produce the best people.

### Q&A demo moments

- The view-as switch: internal documents disappear for a candidate.
- The identity-swap fairness check.
- A manager's "under 30" remark being flagged and kept out of requirements.

## 12. Actions only the team can take

1. Rotate the exposed secrets on all environments: the proxy API key, `SECRET_KEY`, MySQL and admin passwords.
2. Warn every member, and the VPS operator, before Block 1 merges: removing `.env` from git makes the next `git pull` delete the local file. Back it up first. Branches `deploy` and `hoangthanh` are affected.
3. Get API keys for the chosen AI vendors before Block 2. Gemini's free tier allows Google to use content to improve its products, so it is for sample data only. Real candidate data requires a paid tier.
4. Interview real HR staff at Korean companies.
5. Review and edit the sample company documents, which subagents draft, to meet the contest's team-contribution rule.
6. Record 10 to 15 voice clips for the speech-to-text benchmark at the start of Block 3.
7. Ask the organiser about the scoring conflict and the submission link.

## 13. Open risks

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| Strong candidates abandon an AI-first gate | Short session, Fit Compass first, completion rate measured per channel, HR fast-track |
| Speech-to-text quality for Vietnamese and Korean is unverified; the vendor pages read do not name them | Benchmark first in Block 3 |
| `edge-tts` is unofficial and can stop working | `tts` is a configurable slot |
| Mandatory session cost scales with every CV | Session cap of 15 minutes, measured cost per session |
| Voice reveals gender and region to HR | HR reads the transcript first and plays audio on demand |
| Remote session can be coached by another AI | The recording is a signal, not proof; the in-person interview is the final check |
| Scope is large: 19 items | Built in order; Block 3 yields a complete demo |
